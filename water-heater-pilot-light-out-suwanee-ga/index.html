Gas Water Heater Pilot Light Keeps Going Out in Suwanee, GA

A pilot that will not stay lit usually points to a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot tube, a draft or a failing gas control valve. A few checks are safe for a homeowner, and a few are not, and the line between them starts with the smell of gas.

If you smell gas, stop reading and leave

If you smell gas, a rotten-egg odor, leave the building right away and move a safe distance. Call the gas utility, for most homes Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321, or 911, once you are outside. Do this before you call a plumber.

Do not try to find the source, relight anything, flip a switch or use a phone or flashlight inside. A pilot that has gone out can let unburned gas collect around the heater, and a spark from a switch is enough to ignite it. Atlanta Gas Light says not to try to stop a leak yourself, and that advice applies to a water heater closet as much as anywhere.

What the pilot and thermocouple actually do

A standing pilot is a small gas flame that stays lit to ignite the main burner when the tank calls for heat. A thermocouple is a short copper-tipped rod that sits in that flame and produces a tiny electrical signal, about the strength of a battery, that holds the gas valve open.

If the thermocouple stops producing that signal because it is worn, coated with soot or poorly positioned, the valve closes and the pilot goes out. That is why a pilot can light when you hold the button down and die the moment you let go.

What the pilot is telling you

How the pilot behaves points to the cause before anyone touches a tool.

Lights only while the button is held

The thermocouple is not generating enough signal to hold the valve open, and it is the most common culprit.

Goes out hours or days later

A draft, a clogged pilot tube or a weak gas supply can let the flame die slowly.

Yellow or flickering flame

A healthy pilot burns blue. Yellow or lazy flames suggest dirt or poor air supply.

Goes out when the furnace or dryer runs

Competing appliances can pull air away from the water heater flue and disturb the flame.

Soot or scorch marks at the base

This is a combustion warning sign, and it means stop and call before relighting.

Safe homeowner checks versus calls for a pro

The left column is limited to what the heater's own label allows a homeowner to do. The right column is where a licensed plumber should take over.

Safe to try yourself

  • Confirm there is no gas smell before anything else
  • Read the relight instructions printed on the heater label and follow them exactly
  • Wait the full time the label states before relighting, so gas can clear
  • Relight once, then note whether it holds
  • Vacuum dust and lint from around the base with the unit off
  • Check that nothing is stored against the heater or blocking the air supply

Call a licensed plumber

  • Any gas smell at all, after leaving and calling the gas utility
  • A pilot that goes out a second time after a careful relight
  • A yellow, sooty or lifting flame, or black marks around the base
  • A pilot that lights only while the button is held down
  • Replacing a thermocouple, thermopile or gas control valve
  • Any work on gas piping, vent pipe or the burner assembly

Why a lit pilot will not stay lit

The usual causes fall into a short list. Each has a different fix, and only some are cheap.

Worn or dirty thermocouple

This is the most common cause. The rod can lose output with age or collect soot, and replacing it is a standard repair for a plumber with the right wrench and a gas leak test afterward.

Clogged pilot tube or orifice

The pilot tube is a thin line that feeds gas to the flame. Dust and rust flakes can narrow it, which starves the flame until it flickers out.

Draft or poor combustion air

A blocked flue, a backdrafting vent or a tight utility room can disturb the flame. On newer tanks with a sealed combustion door, lint on the air screen at the base is a common culprit.

Failing gas control valve

The valve holds the safety and temperature controls in one unit. When it fails, the pilot may not stay lit even with a new thermocouple, and the usual answer is valve replacement or a new heater.

A careful relight, in order

Use these steps only when there is no gas smell, and only if the label on your heater gives matching instructions. The label wins over this page.

  1. Check for gas

    Sniff near the heater and floor. Any odor means leave and call, do not continue.

  2. Turn the control to pilot

    Set the gas knob as the label directs and wait the stated time before trying again.

  3. Light and hold

    Hold the pilot button down for the time the label states, then release slowly.

  4. Watch for stability

    The flame should be steady and blue. If it dies, stop and call instead of repeating.

Suwanee note: who handles what when the hot water stops

Atlanta Gas Light publishes 877.427.4321 as its 24-hour emergency line and says to leave the area, avoid ignition sources like phones and light switches, and call from a safe place. We cannot confirm from its pages that it serves every Suwanee address, so look at your gas bill and call the utility named there if it differs.

Gwinnett County Water Resources says it does not control the temperature of the water in your home, and for a lack of hot water the cause is likely the water heater. The pilot problem is yours and your plumber's to solve.

Georgia's plumbing code amendments say gas-fired water heaters and their venting fall under the International Fuel Gas Code. If the pilot problem ends in a new heater, the City of Suwanee lists water heater replacement among the work needing a building permit, and the inspections line is 770-945-8996.

What affects the repair price

A pilot repair can be minor or a sign the heater is near the end of its life. These are the factors that decide which.

The cause

A thermocouple swap is simpler than a gas valve replacement.

Age of the heater

An older tank may cost more to repair than the repair is worth.

Venting condition

A corroded or blocked flue has to be corrected before the heater is put back to work.

Access

A tight closet slows the work.

Timing

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls can be priced differently from a booked slot.

Homeowners insurance generally covers sudden water damage, not wear on a heater part like a thermocouple. Check the policy for how it treats appliance age.
