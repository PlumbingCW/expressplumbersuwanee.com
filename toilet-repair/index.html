Toilet overflowing or water on the bathroom floor

If a toilet is overflowing, leaking at the base or flooding a bathroom, call the 24/7 line now. Toilet repair in Suwanee, GA is dispatched at night, on weekends and on holidays, and you can slow the water yourself while you wait.

Reach behind the toilet to the small oval valve near the floor, the supply stop, and turn it clockwise until it stops. That cuts the water refilling the tank and usually ends an overflow that started with a stuck fill valve.

If the water is dirty and coming up from the drain rather than the tank, the problem is a blockage and not the toilet itself. Stop flushing and keep people away from the wet floor.

  1. Close the supply stop behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  2. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or lift the float, to stop the tank refilling.
  3. Do not flush again. Each flush adds water that has nowhere to go.
  4. Towel up standing water and keep anyone away from wet outlets or power strips near the floor.
  5. If the stop will not turn, close the main shutoff for the house and call.

Why a toilet keeps running, and what it wastes

A running toilet is almost always one of three parts in the tank. The flapper that seals the bottom of the tank, the fill valve that refills it, or a chain or float set wrong so the tank never fully closes.

Gwinnett County Water Resources says a leaking toilet is most often a faulty flapper, and that a toilet running constantly can waste 200 gallons or more a day. The EPA's WaterSense program agrees that most toilet leaks come from old or worn flappers.

A toilet that hisses or refills on its own deserves a look this week.

Flapper and chain

A rubber flapper hardens and warps over the years. A chain that is too short holds it slightly open, and one that is too long can slip under it.

A dye test you can do before calling anyone

Gwinnett County Water Resources recommends a dye test for silent leaks. It costs nothing but half an hour.

  • Remove the tank lid and drop in about a teaspoon of food coloring, or a dye tablet.
  • Do not flush for 30 minutes.
  • Look at the bowl. Any color showing means the flapper is leaking into it.

DWR gives out free toilet leak detection kits with dye tablets. The conservation line is 678.376.6722.

What people describe when a toilet needs more than a jiggle

Water around the base

A puddle that returns after you wipe it up often means a worn wax ring or a loose flange, not a cracked bowl.

The toilet rocks

Any rocking when you sit puts stress on the seal and can crack the flange over time, and it gets worse, not better.

Runs for a minute after each flush

The tank is not sealing, which is the flapper or fill valve at work.

Weak flush or gurgling

A partial blockage in the trap, or a vent or drain issue, so a fix to the toilet alone may not help.

Stain on the ceiling below

A leak at the base can travel through the subfloor, so treat a stain under a bathroom as urgent.

Rocking, flanges and wax rings

A toilet sits on a closet flange, the ring bolted to the floor over the drain pipe, and a wax ring seals the gap between them. If the flange is below the finished floor, cracked, or sits on soft subfloor, the toilet rocks and the seal breaks.

That is why the bowl is lifted off for a base leak. The plumber checks the flange, replaces the wax ring, and looks at the subfloor for soft wood from a long-term leak.

Over-tightening the closet bolts cracks the porcelain, so they are snugged evenly in turns.

Fix it yourself or call a plumber

Some tank repairs are routine homeowner work. Others lead to water damage if done wrong.

Reasonable to try yourself

  • Replacing a flapper or adjusting the chain
  • A plunger on a simple clog, with the stop ready

Better with a plumber

  • A toilet that rocks or leaks at the base
  • Sewage on the floor or a repeat backup
  • A cracked bowl or tank
  • Replacing the toilet when the flange or floor is damaged

Repair or replace the toilet

The flapper and fill valve are cheap parts, so most running toilets are repaired. Replacement earns its place in other cases.

Putting in a new toilet

Installing a toilet starts with the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the drain center. Many toilets are built for a 12-inch rough-in, but older homes vary, and a wrong match means the new bowl will not sit flush to the wall.

The old toilet comes up, the flange is inspected, a new wax ring or seal goes on, and the new bowl is set, bolted evenly and connected to a new supply line. Then it is flushed and checked for leaks at the base.

The City of Suwanee lists plumbing changes as work needing a building permit. For a straight swap of a toilet in the same spot, permit rules vary, so ask the city at 770-945-8996 or ask your installer.

When the toilet is not the real problem

A toilet that clogs again and again may point to the drain behind it. If more than one fixture gurgles, read about drain cleaning, and for a line that keeps failing, a sewer camera inspection shows what a plunger cannot.

Gwinnett County Water Resources says the homeowner owns the private sewer lateral, so a backup that starts at the street connection is yours to clear unless it is the county main. If neighbors are backing up too, DWR's 24/7 dispatch is 678.376.7000.

Homeowners near Duluth can call the same number.
