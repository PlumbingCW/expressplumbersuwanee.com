Where Is the Main Water Shutoff Valve in a Suwanee, GA Home?

Most Suwanee homes have three layers of shutoff: the meter valve in the front yard, a main valve where the line enters the house, and small stop valves under each fixture. Finding all three before a leak starts is the cheapest plumbing prep there is.

Three places to shut the water, from smallest to biggest

The right valve depends on how big the leak is. A dripping toilet supply needs only its own stop valve. A burst pipe needs the house main. If the house main will not close, the last resort is the meter valve in the yard.

Learn all three while nothing is wrong. A valve that has not moved in ten years may refuse to turn the first time you need it, and finding that out during a leak is the worst timing.

Find them in this order

Walk the house once with a flashlight. It takes about fifteen minutes.

  1. The meter box in the front yard

    Gwinnett County Water Resources describes the meter as sitting out in the front yard under a metal cover. Lift the lid with a screwdriver, keeping fingers clear, and look for the meter and a valve next to it.

  2. The house main valve

    Look where the water line enters the house, often a garage wall, a utility room, a crawl space or a basement. It is usually a round handle (gate valve) or a lever (ball valve) on the pipe just after the line comes through the wall.

  3. Fixture stop valves

    Check under every sink and behind each toilet for a small oval or round handle on the supply line. A toilet stop is often at the wall, and the washing machine has two valves on the wall box.

  4. The water heater

    The cold supply to the heater has its own valve, which lets you work on the heater without shutting down the whole house.

  5. Label them

    Tag each valve with tape. A guest or a tired family member should not need a plumber to figure it out.

Valve types and how they close

Two valve styles show up on main lines. They close in different ways and fail in different ways.

Ball valveShort lever handleQuarter turn, handle crosswise to the pipeHandle can feel loose if the stem is worn

If any valve will not move with moderate effort, stop. Forcing a 20-year-old gate valve can break it and make the leak bigger.

How to close the meter valve safely

The meter valve is the one most homeowners never touch. Gwinnett County Water Resources describes the curb stop in its freezing-weather guidance, saying to turn the curb stop clockwise so that the holes match. Once they line up, the water is off at the meter.

Use a meter key or a pair of pliers made for the job, not a hammer. Wear gloves, because meter boxes collect spiders and insects. If the lid is stuck, or the valve does not move, leave it alone and call the county dispatch line.

Suwanee note: what the county handles and what it does not

Gwinnett County Water Resources says it is responsible for the water main, the small pipe from the main to your meter and the meter itself. Everything on your side of the meter belongs to you. A broken pipe between the meter and your front door is your repair, even though the water is stopped at a county-owned meter.

The county also says its employees are not permitted to work on private property and cannot test for leaks there, and that it is not responsible for repairing leaks on the customer's side of the meter. Call the 24/7 dispatch line at 678.376.7000 if you see water coming from the meter itself or from the county side. For anything on your side, the plumber handles it.

If you do have a leak, Gwinnett's customer FAQ says it offers adjustments on eligible water and sewer accounts once in a twelve-month period, with the repair done within three months of the increased consumption. Keep the plumber's invoice and photos.

Winter prep: valves, drips and cabinet doors

Gwinnett County Water Resources notes that Georgia does not drop below freezing very often, but when it does, water in home plumbing can expand and break pipes. These steps use the same advice the county gives.

  • Know where your house main is before the first cold snap
  • Drip a faucet overnight when a hard freeze is forecast
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls when temperatures dip below 32 degrees
  • Disconnect garden hoses and cover outdoor spigots
  • If a pipe does freeze, shut the main before it thaws, because the break shows up when the ice melts

A pipe that has frozen once may be cracked even if it is not leaking yet. Watch for a drop in pressure or damp drywall for a day or two after the thaw.

Close it yourself or call

Closing a valve is something every adult in the house should be able to do. Replacing a bad one is a different job.

You can do

  • Close fixture stops under sinks and toilets
  • Close a house main that turns freely
  • Label and photograph valves
  • Exercise valves once a year by turning them gently

Call a plumber for

  • A main valve that will not close or leaks at the stem
  • A seized fixture stop
  • Replacing an old gate valve with a ball valve
  • Water that keeps running after the main is closed

What to do once the water is off

Open the lowest faucet in the house and the highest to drain the pipes and relieve pressure. If a pipe has burst, switch off breakers to wet electrical areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. When in doubt, stay out.
