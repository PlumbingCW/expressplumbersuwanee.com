A faucet or valve that will not shut off

If a faucet will not stop, a supply line is spraying, or a valve handle snapped off, call the 24/7 line. Faucet repair in Suwanee, GA is dispatched at night and on weekends, because water that cannot be stopped does damage by the hour.

Look under the sink for the two small shutoff valves, called angle stops, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each one clockwise until it stops. If they are stuck, corroded or missing, go to the main shutoff for the house.

The main valve is usually where the water line enters the house. The meter itself sits in the front yard under a metal cover, and Gwinnett County Water Resources says everything on the customer side of it is the homeowner's to fix.

  1. Close both angle stops under the sink, clockwise, hot and cold.
  2. If a stop will not close or leaks around the stem, shut the main valve for the house.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the line, then keep it open.
  4. Keep people and electronics away from spray, especially near outlets.
  5. Photograph any damage for the insurance claim, then call.

Faucet problems, in the words people use

Not every drip is an emergency, but each tells you something about what is wearing out.

A steady drip from the spout

A worn washer, seat, cartridge or O-ring. The leak is small now and wastes water around the clock.

Water pooling under the sink

The leak may be a supply line, the faucet base or the drain. Dry the cabinet, wipe the lines and watch where drops return.

Handle stiff, loose or hard to turn off

Mineral buildup or a worn cartridge. A handle that has to be forced is close to failing.

Low flow from one faucet only

Most often a clogged aerator or a partly closed angle stop, not a pipe problem.

Spray from the side or base

Seals at the body have gone. This faucet is usually replaced, not repaired.

What is inside your faucet

A faucet is a valve with a decorative shell. What matters for repair is the type of valve inside, because each one fails differently.

Compression faucets use a rubber washer pressed on a seat and have separate hot and cold handles. They drip when the washer wears. Cartridge and ceramic disk faucets, the single-handle kind, seal with a replaceable cartridge, so the fix is a swap, not a rebuild.

Older ball-type faucets have many small parts and are commonly replaced outright. A good plumber will tell you whether parts for your faucet are still easy to find, or whether replacement is cheaper than repair.

Outdoor faucets, hose bibs and freezing nights

Outside spigots fail differently. A hose left attached through a freeze can burst the pipe behind the wall, and the leak only shows when the spigot is turned on in spring.

Gwinnett County Water Resources notes that Georgia temperatures do not drop below freezing very often, but when they do, water in home plumbing can expand and break pipes. DWR advises dripping a faucet overnight and opening cabinet doors when temperatures dip below 32 degrees.

Take hoses off before a cold night. If a spigot drips from the stem or the vacuum breaker cap, it can often be repaired, but a spigot that leaks inside the wall needs a plumber and burst pipe repair if the pipe split.

Low flow and the aerator kit

A faucet that suddenly runs weak often just has a clogged aerator, the small screen on the spout tip. Unscrew it, rinse out the grit, and you are done.

Gwinnett County Water Resources hands out free low-flow retrofit kits at its Central Facility. Each kit has a low-flow showerhead, faucet aerators and toilet leak detection dye tablets, so a fresh aerator may cost you only a trip to pick one up.

Georgia's plumbing code limits bathroom faucets to 1.5 gallons per minute and kitchen faucets to 2.0 in new construction, so those are good targets if you swap fixtures. If low flow hits every faucet, the pressure reducing valve is the homeowner's, and a plumber should check it with a gauge.
