What a water heater repair actually involves

A residential storage water heater is an insulated steel tank, usually 40 or 50 gallons, with a heat source, a thermostat, a dip tube that feeds cold water to the bottom, and a temperature and pressure relief valve on the side or top. Repair means replacing the failed part or clearing the failed condition without replacing the whole tank.

Most of the parts that fail are cheap compared to the tank. A bad thermostat, a burned-out heating element, a worn thermocouple or a weeping drain valve can all be changed in one visit. The tank itself, once it leaks through the shell, cannot be patched.

What you are seeing and what it usually means

Callers describe the same handful of symptoms, and each one narrows down the cause before anyone opens a panel.

Water around the base

Check the source before blaming the tank. A leaking supply connector, a relief valve discharge or a dripping drain valve is repairable. Water seeping from under the jacket is not.

No hot water at all

On gas, this is often a pilot that went out or a thermocouple that no longer senses flame. On electric, look for a tripped breaker or a failed upper element or thermostat.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast

A dead lower element on an electric unit, or a dip tube that has broken down, leaves only a few gallons of truly hot water at the top of the tank.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment on the bottom steel traps steam bubbles under it. It means the heater is working harder than it should and usually calls for a flush.

Rust-colored hot water

If only the hot side runs brown, the tank interior or a spent anode rod is the likely source. Cold taps running the same color point to the pipes instead.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters fail in different places

The fuel type decides where we look first, so tell the dispatcher which kind of heater you own. It is stamped on the data plate on the side of the tank.

Gas storage heaters

A gas unit has a burner at the bottom, a flue running up the middle and a gas control valve on the front. The usual failures are a dirty pilot assembly, a worn thermocouple, a clogged burner tube or a draft problem in the vent that pushes flame out of the combustion chamber.

Gas heaters and their venting fall under the fuel gas code, not the plumbing chapter. A repair that touches the burner, the gas line or the flue is gas work, and a smell of gas means you leave first and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone else.

Electric storage heaters

Most electric tanks carry two 240-volt heating elements, one near the top and one near the bottom, each with its own thermostat. The upper element fires first and the lower one finishes the job, so a failure at either level gives a different symptom.

A tripped high-limit reset button on the upper thermostat is common after a dry fire. If it trips again after being pressed once, stop pressing it and call.

Hybrid heat pump heaters

A hybrid unit has a heat pump on top that pulls heat out of the surrounding air, plus conventional elements as backup. Its repair list is longer than a plain electric tank: fan, compressor, air filter, condensate drain and the controls.

These units need open air around them and a clear condensate path. A clogged condensate line or a dirty filter often explains a hybrid that has quietly switched to its slower element mode.

Soft water, sediment and the anode rod

Gwinnett County Water Resources lists total hardness for Gwinnett water at about 1.05 to 1.87 grains per gallon, with an average of 1.34, which is very soft water. Heavy limescale is therefore a smaller part of the story here than in hard-water cities, though sediment still settles over the years.

The anode rod is the sacrificial metal rod screwed into the top of the tank. It corrodes so the steel does not, and once it is eaten down to the core wire, the tank starts rusting from the inside. Checking it on a tank that is five or six years old is the cheapest way to add years to a heater that is still sound.

A flush clears loose sediment through the drain valve at the bottom. On a heater that has never been flushed, an old drain valve can fail or clog on the first attempt, so a plumber may recommend leaving a stuck valve alone.

Safety parts you should not tamper with

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the last safeguard if the thermostat fails. A discharge pipe runs from it toward the floor, and that pipe must never be capped or plugged. A valve that drips regularly may point to thermal expansion in a closed system or to a worn seat, and either one needs a plumber instead of a plug.

Homes with a pressure reducing valve or a backflow preventer on the supply are closed systems. Gwinnett County Water Resources says the pressure reducing valve is the homeowner's responsibility, and the county does not send people to look at it. A storage heater on a closed system needs a thermal expansion tank, and a dead one can show up as a relief valve that spits water after every heating cycle.
