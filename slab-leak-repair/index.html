Suspected slab leak: what to do right now

If a floor is warm for no reason, water is rising at a baseboard, or the meter spins with everything off, call the 24/7 line. Slab leak service in Suwanee, GA is available at night and on weekends, and a same-day visit is offered when the schedule allows.

Until someone arrives, close the main shut-off valve. A pressurized line under concrete keeps pushing water into the soil and up through cracks, so stopping the supply limits the damage to flooring, walls and cabinets.

A slab leak is a leak in a water supply line that runs inside or beneath a poured concrete floor. It is the pressurized hot or cold line that fails here, not the drain.

  1. Close the main shut-off valve at the house.
  2. Keep people and pets off wet floors near outlets, power strips and the electrical panel.
  3. Lift rugs and move furniture legs off wet carpet or wood.
  4. Run the meter test (below) so you can tell the plumber what the meter did.

If your home sits on a concrete slab

Not every house has this problem, because not every house has pipes in or under concrete. If your floor plan is slab on grade, the supply lines may run through the slab or in the soil just beneath it. A basement home or a crawlspace home usually has exposed pipes that are easy to reach, and the page on leak detection fits those better.

Slab leaks come from the pipe itself, the soil around it or the way it was installed. Pipes can rub against rock or concrete as a house settles, hot lines expand and contract all day, and a bad joint or a pinhole can weep for months before it shows.

The city's own plan says about 60 percent of Suwanee homes were built in 2000 or later. In homes of that age, ask your plumber what material the supply lines are, because the material changes the repair choice.

What a slab leak sounds, feels and looks like

Most slab leaks announce themselves in odd ways well before water reaches the surface.

A warm patch on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab can heat the concrete above it. Walk barefoot across the room and compare.

Water running sound with everything off

A hiss or rush near a wall or floor, even faint, is pressurized water escaping.

A water bill that climbs with no change in use

Gwinnett County Water Resources notes that high bills are often caused by a leak inside or outside the home, and a slab leak is a steady loss.

Damp carpet, lifting tile or musty odor

Moisture finds the path of least resistance, which is often a baseboard or a slab crack.

Low hot water pressure

A drop on the hot side only can point to a leak in the hot line before it reaches the fixtures.

How the leak is found before anyone cuts concrete

A slab is expensive to open, so the work starts with proof. The sequence below is why the location comes first.

  1. Run the meter test

    Gwinnett County Water Resources describes it: no water use for two hours, read the meter before and after. If the number moved or the leak wheel kept turning, water is escaping somewhere.

  2. Isolate hot and cold

    Closing the water heater valve and checking pressure on each side tells the technician which line is leaking.

  3. Listen and scan

    Acoustic listening picks up the hiss through the floor, and thermal readings can show a warm trail on the surface.

  4. Mark the spot

    The technician marks the floor above the leak so any opening is as small as possible.

What drives the price of a slab leak repair

No two slab leaks match. The price depends on a handful of things you can see and a few you cannot until the leak is found.

Depth and location

A leak near a wall is easier to reach than one in the middle of a room.

The floor covering

Tile, hardwood and carpet each cost different amounts to remove and put back.

Number of leaks

One failure is a repair. A line with several suggests a reroute.

Pipe material and age

Older or brittle pipe can crack where it is touched.

Time of day

A suspected slab leak that surfaces overnight is handled as an emergency call, which can cost differently from a daytime booking.

Home insurance often covers sudden accidental water damage but not the cost of repairing the pipe itself, and policies differ on whether they pay to reach a hidden leak. Read the policy and call the agent early.

Why a small pinhole can do big damage

Household supply lines run at pressure, so even a leak the size of a pinhole sends water out steadily. A leak that loses a gallon per minute is 1,440 gallons a day. Under concrete, that water has nowhere to go but sideways and up, which is why soft spots and bubbling flooring show up long after the leak began.
