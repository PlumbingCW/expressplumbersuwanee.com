Water coming through a wall or ceiling right now

In an attached home, water travels, so act on your own unit first.

  1. Close the main water valve for your unit, or the shutoff under the fixture that is leaking.
  2. Keep people away from wet outlets and light fixtures, and stay clear of the electrical panel if it is wet.
  3. If water is dripping from a neighbor's ceiling or wall, knock and tell them so they can close their own valve.
  4. Smell gas? Get everyone out, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is and what you have shut off.

What Suwanee Station is

According to the City of Suwanee's 2040 plan, Suwanee Station was master planned in the early 2000s as a mixed-use, transit-oriented neighborhood, in anticipation of a commuter rail station. The plan called for 1,186 housing units, and 1,107 have been built.

Those include two multifamily developments, attached townhomes and detached single-family homes on small lots. The plan calls the area relatively dense, with a mix of housing types and a connected, walkable street network.

A real estate directory lists three communities under the Suwanee Station name: Suwanee Station, The Abbey at Suwanee Station and The Enclave at Suwanee Station. We use those only as supporting evidence, because the city's plan is the source for what the area contains.

What the planned rail station means for you

The plan says the city owns the last undeveloped tract here, about 12 acres along the Norfolk Southern railroad. It is reserved for a commuter rail station, and the plan expects mostly non-residential uses there if a station arrives.

The plan also says commercial uses have been built along the edge facing Peachtree Industrial Boulevard, with no substantial commercial inside the neighborhood itself. For a homeowner, none of this changes the plumbing, but it explains why the area reads as a residential pocket.

Shared walls, shared lines

In townhomes and apartments, a leak can reach another home quickly, so knowing your own shutoff matters more than it does in a detached house. Find it today, and see that everyone in the household knows where it is.

If several neighbors have the same slow drain or backup, the blockage may sit in a line you do not own. We can tell you where your own line ends, and drain cleaning clears a blocked branch line inside the unit.

Signs worth a call in an attached or small-lot home

A damp spot on a wall or ceiling shared with a neighbor

Close your unit's shutoff and see if it stops. Leak detection can trace which line is the source.

Hot water that fades or smells off

A tank heater near the end of its life can leak without warning. Water heater repair comes first, and replacement if the tank itself is leaking.

A meter that moves with everything off

Read it, wait two hours with no water use, and read it again. Movement means a hidden leak somewhere past the meter.

A toilet or faucet that runs all day

Small leaks add up on the water bill. Toilet repair and faucet repair are usually small jobs.

A water heater tucked in a closet

Find out where yours sits and clear the area, because a leaking tank has little room to spread safely.

Permits and the call before a dig

The City of Suwanee lists changes to plumbing among the work that needs a building permit, and it lists water heater replacement among permitted work. Its inspections line is 770-945-8996, and one call tells you what your project needs. The Georgia state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code with Georgia amendments, so a plumber doing permitted work follows that code.

In an association community, read your own rules before you start, because an association may require approval on top of the city's permit. Outdoor digging, even for a small line, needs a Georgia 811 ticket filed at least three business days ahead.
