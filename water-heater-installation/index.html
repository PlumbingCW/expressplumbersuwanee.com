What installation covers and where it differs from a swap

Water heater installation is the work of setting a heater, connecting its water, fuel or power, venting any exhaust and testing the result. It covers a first-time heater in a new space, a change of fuel, a move from one room to another and a planned upgrade from an aging tank that still works.

A like-for-like swap on a failed tank is a narrower job, handled under water heater replacement. Installation is the broader category, and because nothing is flooding, you have time to choose the unit instead of taking whatever is on the shelf.

Decide these before anyone picks up a wrench

Most delays on a planned install come from questions that could have been answered a week earlier.

  • Fuel: natural gas, electric or a hybrid heat pump unit. The data plate on the current heater shows what you have today.
  • Size: first hour rating and tank volume, from the EnergyGuide label, not the gallon number alone.
  • Location: garage, closet, attic or basement. Floor load, headroom and a drain pan path all depend on it.
  • Venting: where the flue or exhaust will leave the house for gas models, and whether the old flue can be reused.
  • Drainage: where the relief valve discharge pipe and any condensate line will run.
  • Supply pressure: whether the house has a pressure reducing valve or backflow preventer, which makes it a closed system.

Photograph the data plate and the area around the heater before you call. It saves a round of questions.

Fuel and type at a glance

Each type trades installed cost, running cost and what the house has to provide. The table sums up the differences an installer will talk through.

Which one is right depends on the house, not on the label. Ask the installer to explain the connection work for each option.

Sizing, placement and the code behind both

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code sizes residential water heaters by first hour rating, matched to bedrooms and bathrooms in a table, or by the manufacturer's recommendations. The code does not name a single tank size for every house, so the label on the heater is the number to read.

Gas heaters and venting

Gas distribution piping, gas-fired water heaters and their venting systems are regulated under the fuel gas code. A heater that vents poorly can push carbon monoxide into the house, so the flue slope, the connector size and the draft get checked on every gas install.

If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call the gas utility, for most homes Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321, or 911, before calling a plumber.

Closed systems and expansion tanks

A house with a pressure reducing valve, check valve or backflow preventer is a closed system, and a storage heater on that kind of supply needs a thermal expansion control device. Georgia's amended code asks for it and sets it at least 18 inches of pipe from the heater's cold inlet.

The pressure reducing valve is the homeowner's, according to Gwinnett County Water Resources, so a planned install is a good time to have it tested with a gauge.

How a planned install is laid out

Because nothing is leaking, the visit is organized and paced.

  1. Walk-through

    The installer looks at the space, reads the data plate and checks gas, electric, vent and drain routes.

  2. Quote and permit plan

    You hear the clear price and how the city permit will be handled.

  3. Rough-in changes

    A new circuit, gas stub, vent run or drain pan is put in before the heater is set.

  4. Set and connect

    The tank or tankless unit is placed, connected and fitted with a relief valve and discharge pipe.

  5. Test and walk-through

    Heat cycle, pressure, leak and venting checks are done, then you are shown the shutoffs and the thermostat.

Why installation prices differ from house to house

Two homes asking for the same 50 gallon heater can get very different numbers. The clear price covers equipment, labor and the code-required parts, and you hear it before work starts.

Equipment

Standard tank, high-recovery gas, heat pump and tankless models sit in very different price tiers.

Fuel changes

Converting from gas to electric or the reverse means new circuits, gas lines or venting.

Relocation

Moving the heater means new supply, drain and vent runs.

Required add-ons

Drain pan, expansion tank, sediment trap and new shutoff valves are common code items.

Permit and inspection

Permit fees and inspection scheduling vary by city and can add days.

Planned installs are not covered by home insurance since they are maintenance and upgrades, not sudden accidental damage. Read the policy if you are unsure what it covers.

Reasons homeowners plan a new heater

The tank is a decade old

A heater past ten years is cheaper to replace on your schedule than at midnight with a flooded garage.

Hot water runs out too fast

A bigger household or a new soaking tub can outgrow the original tank.

A remodel moves the laundry or bath

New space needs a heater location, venting and drain routes planned from the start.

You are choosing tankless

The tankless water heater option needs gas, vent and electrical planning before the unit is bought.

Permits, inspections and asking the right questions

The City of Suwanee building permits page lists plumbing changes and water heater replacement as work that needs a building permit, and gives 770-945-8996 as the inspections number. Ask the installer how the permit will be pulled and when the inspection will happen.

Permit rules for other cities, including Lawrenceville, differ, so ask the city for your address. A heater that is working hard and showing its age may also be a candidate for water heater repair first, if the tank is otherwise sound.
