Emergency backup: the main line is full again

If sewage is rising in a tub or floor drain and the last cable run only bought a week, call the 24/7 line. Emergency hydro jetting in Suwanee, GA is dispatched at night and on weekends, and jetting is often the next step when a line keeps refilling.

Until the technician is on the way, treat every drain in the house as closed. A single load of laundry sends about 20 gallons or more toward a line that has no room for it.

A backup that reaches the floor is a sanitation problem as much as a plumbing one, so keep the affected room clear and the wet materials separate from the rest of the house.

  1. Stop the washer, dishwasher and showers.
  2. Do not flush, even once more.
  3. Keep kids and pets out of any room with surfaced sewage.
  4. Unplug or lift anything electrical off a wet floor, and leave the breaker alone if you would have to stand in water to reach it.
  5. Find the outdoor cleanout cap so the technician can start there.

What a water jet does that a cable cannot

Hydro jetting is a cleaning method that pushes water through a hose at very high pressure, rated in psi, out of a nozzle with several small jets. Some jets point backward to pull the hose forward through the line, and some spray at an angle to strip the wall.

A cable auger bores a hole through a clog. A jet washes the full circle of the pipe, which is why a line can stay open for much longer afterward.

Grease and soap scale

Cooking grease cools in the pipe and builds a waxy layer that narrows a 4 inch line to something closer to a straw. Hot, pressurized water breaks the layer down and flushes it toward the sewer instead of leaving a ring behind.

Roots and fine debris

Fine hair roots that enter at a joint can be cut back by a chain-style or spinning nozzle. Jetting clears the growth but does not seal the opening, so roots tend to return if the joint stays open.

Sand, silt and wipe clumps

Gritty sediment that settles in a low spot is moved along by flow, not hooked by a cable. Wipes labeled flushable bunch up in the same low spots, and a jet breaks the mats apart.

Jetting against cabling, camera work and doing nothing

Jetting is not always the right call, and a line in poor shape can be hurt by it. This comparison shows where each approach fits.

A technician will not jet a cracked, collapsed or badly offset pipe. In that case the camera finding changes the plan.

Backups that point toward jetting

Repeat trouble tells you more than a single event does. These are the patterns that usually mean buildup, not a one-time clog.

Cleared and clogged again

A line that opens with a cable and slows within a month or two is usually coated, not just blocked.

Slow drains in every bathroom

When the tub, sink and toilet all lag together, the shared line has lost diameter.

Gurgling and sewer odor

Air pushed past a partial blockage bubbles back through traps and brings a smell with it.

Greasy kitchen line

A kitchen sink that never drains cleanly after years of pans being rinsed hot is a common candidate.

Who pays for the line, and what grease has to do with it

Gwinnett County Water Resources says the property owner maintains the private sewer lateral, which runs from the house to the edge of the right-of-way. Its fats, oil and grease page adds that most FOG-related overflows happen on residential sewer lines, and that a clog on your property is yours to take care of.

In practice, a jetting visit usually starts at the cleanout. The county maintains the one at the right-of-way, and the homeowner is responsible for the one set closer to the house, so the technician works through whichever you can reach.

If the street main looks like the problem, for example when neighbors report the same backup, call Gwinnett County Water Resources 24/7 dispatch at 678.376.7000. The county does not enter private property, so a clog in your own pipe still falls to you.

Many Suwanee homes are on county sewer, but some older areas use septic. Tell us which one you have, because a septic line and its tank call for a different plan than a jet run to the county connection.

What moves the price of a jetting visit

Jetting takes a heavier machine and more water than a cable run, and the final number reflects what the line needs. You hear the price before work starts.

Length and diameter of the line

A short 3 inch branch is a smaller job than a long 4 inch main out to the connection.

How coated the pipe is

Years of grease can take more than one pass at different nozzle settings.

Cleanout access

A clean outdoor cap is quick, while entry through a toilet flange takes more setup and care.

Camera pass

A look before and after shows whether the pipe is sound, and it adds time.

Night or weekend call

A backup at midnight is an emergency visit, and its rate can differ from a jetting appointment you schedule ahead.

Home insurance often pays for sudden water damage, but cleaning a line is usually treated as maintenance, and sewer backup coverage is commonly a separate add-on. Check your policy before you need it.

Before the jet hose goes in

A few minutes of preparation keeps the job short and protects your floors.

  • Locate the cleanout cap, usually a round plastic cap near the foundation or at the lot edge.
  • Clear shrubs, mulch or a planter from around it.
  • Note which drains are slow and which are fully stopped.
  • Mention any earlier camera work, a past root problem, or sections of older pipe.
  • Ask the technician to show you what came out of the line.

Jetting a pipe that is already cracked can make a bad section worse, so a camera look first is a reasonable ask. If the picture shows damage, the conversation moves to repair of the damaged section. A technician can also handle a simple clog through standard sewer line cleaning when the pipe wall is clean.
