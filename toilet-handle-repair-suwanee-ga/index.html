Toilet Handle Repair in Suwanee, GA

A toilet handle that wobbles, sticks down or flops with no resistance is usually a ten minute fix at the tank. Here is how to tell which part has failed and when the handle is hiding a bigger problem in Suwanee, GA.

The short answer on a bad toilet handle

Most toilet handle problems come down to one of three parts: the handle nut, the lever arm inside the tank, or the chain that connects the arm to the flapper. A loose handle is a tight nut away from fixed. A handle that feels limp or must be held down usually has a chain that is too long, unhooked or snapped.

Lift the tank lid, flush once and watch. Whatever moves wrong in the first two seconds tells you which part to replace, and the parts are cheap hardware store items that fit most standard tanks.

What the handle is telling you

How the handle feels when you press it points straight at the failed part.

Handle wobbles side to side

The mounting nut has backed off or the plastic around the hole has worn. Tightening often solves it, a worn nut means a new handle.

Handle goes down with no resistance

The lever arm may have snapped off inside the tank, or the chain has come unhooked from the arm.

Handle sticks down after a flush

The chain is likely catching on something, or the arm is rubbing the tank wall because the handle is crooked.

Weak flush, you have to hold it down

Too much slack in the chain means the flapper lifts only part way and closes early. Too little slack does the opposite and keeps the flapper from sealing.

Handle looks fine but toilet runs

The handle is innocent. Check the flapper and the fill valve.

Fixing a loose or limp handle in six steps

You need a pair of pliers or an adjustable wrench, and a replacement handle only if the old one is cracked or corroded.

  1. Shut the supply

    Turn the oval or round valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops, then flush to empty most of the tank.

  2. Look at the chain

    Open the lid. The chain should hang from the end of the lever arm to the flapper with about half an inch of slack, not a tight line and not a loop that can slip under the flapper.

  3. Tighten the nut

    The mounting nut on the inside of the tank usually has a reverse thread, so turn it counterclockwise to tighten. Snug is enough, since plastic nuts crack when forced.

  4. Replace a broken handle

    Unscrew the nut the other way, pull the handle out through the front of the tank and match the new one to the same side: front, side or angle mount.

  5. Rehook and trim the chain

    Clip the chain to the arm hole that gives half an inch of slack. Extra chain can be trimmed with pliers, but leave a couple of links spare.

  6. Test flush three times

    Turn the water back on and flush. The flapper should lift fully, drop cleanly and the tank should refill without the handle sticking.

Suwanee note: the flapper is the usual leak, and the county hands out free kits

While the lid is off, look at the rubber flapper at the bottom of the tank. Gwinnett County Water Resources says the cause of a leaking toilet is most often a faulty flapper, and that a toilet running constantly could waste 200 gallons of water or more every day. A new handle will not cure that, so a flapper that is soft, warped or slimy is worth replacing in the same visit.

The EPA WaterSense page on residential toilets backs this up, noting that most toilet leaks come from old or worn-out flappers. If your toilet refills on its own now and then when no one has flushed, treat the flapper as the first suspect.

Gwinnett County Water Resources also says free low-flow home retrofit kits are available at its Central Facility. The kit holds a low-flow showerhead, faucet aerators and toilet leak detection dye tablets, so one trip also covers the dye for checking your new flapper seal. The county conservation line for the kits is 678.376.6722, and the county page also gives an email, dwrconserve@gwinnettcounty.com.

Do it yourself or call a plumber

Most handle jobs are homeowner work. The line gets crossed when the problem is not the handle.

Fix it yourself when

  • The handle is loose and the nut simply needs snugging.
  • The chain is unhooked or the wrong length.
  • The handle is cracked, corroded or snapped off and the tank is otherwise dry.
  • You have the right replacement for front, side or angle mount.

Call a plumber when

  • The tank cracks, leaks at the bolts or sweats onto the floor.
  • The flush valve seat is pitted so a new flapper still will not seal.
  • The toilet keeps running after a new flapper and handle.
  • The nut will not turn, the tank is old and a bolt is seized.

If you cannot tell which style you have, take a photo of the inside of the tank to the store.

When a handle repair is really a flush valve problem

Some tanks leave the factory with a flush valve that is a different design from the standard flapper type. A canister or tower style valve has a floating cup instead of a hinged flapper. The handle chain attaches to a cup lift, and the repair kit for it is different from a flapper kit.

If you pull the lid and cannot find a rubber flapper, do not guess at a replacement. Note the brand and model stamped inside the tank, which is usually a small label near the water line.

A toilet that has been rebuilt more than twice with parts that keep failing can be on borrowed time. At that point a plumbing installation visit for a new fixture may cost less than another round of parts, and our toilet repair page explains what a plumber checks first.

Before you buy replacement parts

A two minute check saves a second trip to the store.

  • Write down the tank brand and the handle location: front, side or angle.
  • Check whether the tank has a flapper or a canister style flush valve.
  • Measure the chain from lever arm to flapper while the tank is full.
  • Press the flapper down by hand. If it seals, the old rubber was the issue.
  • Look at the supply line and shutoff for drips while the lid is off.

If the shutoff valve itself will not close, stop. Forcing it can crack the valve stem, and a plumber can swap the valve with the main closed.
