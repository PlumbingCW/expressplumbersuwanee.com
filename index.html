Suwanee homes and what they ask of a plumber

Suwanee was incorporated in 1949 and grew fast after 1990, and the city's own plan says about 60 percent of its housing units date from 2000 or later. For a homeowner, that means most plumbing here is a few decades old at most, which helps when a technician asks how old the water heater or the supply lines are.

Suwanee Creek is known for frequent flooding according to the Gwinnett County hazard plan, and Hurricane Helene closed Martin Farm Road in 2024. If your home sits low or near the creek, ask about a sanitary backwater valve, which Gwinnett County says Georgia plumbing code requires when the lowest fixture sits below the next upstream manhole.

Most Suwanee homes receive water and sewer service from Gwinnett County Water Resources, and a few older areas use septic tanks. That split matters on a service call, because the line you own ends at the county connection.

Water quality and your fixtures

Gwinnett County's drinking water comes from Lake Lanier and is treated at the Shoal Creek or Lanier filter plant, and the county adds chlorine to the supply. The county reports very soft water, about 1.05 to 1.87 grains per gallon.

Soft water means less mineral scale on heating elements and faucet aerators than in hard-water regions. Sediment and anode rod wear still shorten a tank water heater's life, so a water heater repair call is still the right move when the hot water fades.
