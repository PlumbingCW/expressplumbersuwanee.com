50 Gallon Water Heater Installation Cost and Labor in Suwanee, GA

The price of a 50 gallon tank install is the heater plus the labor, parts, permit and haul-away around it, and the labor is where quotes differ most. This page breaks the job into line items so two quotes can be compared fairly.

What a 50 gallon installation quote should cover

A water heater installation quote is the price to remove the old tank, set a new one, reconnect water, gas or electric, vent it if it burns gas, and test it. A 50 gallon tank is the common size for a three or four bedroom house, and the heater itself is often less than half the story.

The rest is labor and small parts: a temperature and pressure relief valve, discharge piping, flexible connectors, a drain pan and a shutoff valve. A quote that lists only the tank leaves you guessing.

How the labor is figured

Labor on a straight swap in an open garage is the easy case. The plumber shuts off water and fuel, drains the old tank through a garden hose, disconnects it, and sets the new one on a pan with fresh connectors.

Time goes up when the heater sits in an attic or a tight closet and has to be carried down. It also goes up when the old tank is corroded in place, the shutoff valve will not close, or the gas line or flue needs correcting before the new unit can run.

Gas versus electric labor

A gas heater adds a gas connection, a leak check at every joint and a vent that has to draw properly. An electric heater skips the vent but needs the right breaker and wire gauge for its element load.

Who should be doing the work

Replacing a heater touches water, fuel and, on gas models, combustion. Ask the installer for a Georgia plumbing license number and look it up through the Georgia Secretary of State license verification site, verify.sos.ga.gov, before work starts.

What moves the final bill

No two installs cost the same because no two water heater closets are the same. These are the factors that change the number.

Fuel and model

A gas tank with a power vent costs differently than an atmospheric gas tank or an electric one.

Access

A garage floor is quick. An attic or basement stairwell is slower and may need extra labor.

Code corrections

Venting, relief piping or gas piping that no longer meets current rules must be fixed as part of the job.

Added parts

An expansion tank, a drain pan or a new shutoff valve add to the parts list.

Timing

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls can be priced differently than a booked weekday slot.

Homeowners insurance generally covers sudden water damage from a burst tank, not the cost of replacing a worn-out heater. Check your policy for how it treats appliance age and failure.

Why a 50 gallon label is not the whole sizing answer

Tank size is only part of how well a heater serves a household. Georgia's state minimum plumbing code now includes a residential sizing table based on first hour rating, which counts how much hot water a unit can deliver in its first hour of heavy use, and it ties that rating to the number of bedrooms and bathrooms.

The first hour rating is printed on the yellow energy label, and it matters more than the gallon number. Ask your installer to size by that rating or the manufacturer's instructions, not by the size already in the closet.

Suwanee note: permits and gas rules before the install

The City of Suwanee building permits page lists plumbing changes and water heater replacement among the work that needs a building permit. The city inspections line, 770-945-8996, can tell you how to schedule it. Ask your installer who pulls the permit and whether it is on the quote, then keep the paperwork for a future sale.

Georgia's state plumbing code amendments, published by the Department of Community Affairs, say gas-fired water heaters and their venting fall under the International Fuel Gas Code instead of the plumbing chapter. In practice that means the vent run and gas connection are checked as their own part of the job, so a quote for a gas unit should mention both.

The same code says a storage water heater needs a thermal expansion control device when its cold supply passes through a check valve, pressure reducing valve or backflow preventer. Gwinnett County Water Resources notes that the home pressure reducing valve belongs to the homeowner, so if your house has one, an expansion tank is likely a line item on your quote.

Per Gwinnett County Water Resources, local water hardness runs about 1.05 to 1.87 grains per gallon, which is very soft. Scale buildup on the tank bottom is therefore a slower problem here than in hard-water areas, so do not let a quote add a water softener to the heater job just to protect it.

What to ask before you accept a quote

These questions turn a number on paper into a job you understand.

  • Is the permit included, and who schedules the city inspection?
  • Does the total include the relief valve, discharge piping, connectors and a drain pan?
  • Is an expansion tank included, and if not, why not?
  • What happens to the old tank, and is haul-away listed?
  • What is the first hour rating of the unit being installed?
  • Will the gas connection and vent be tested before the crew leaves?

Gas smell during an install or any time

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building right away and call the gas utility, for most homes Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321, or 911, from a safe distance. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside or try to find the leak yourself. Call a plumber only after the gas utility says the area is safe.

A smell after a recent install is a reason to leave, not to wait and see.
