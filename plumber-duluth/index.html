What Duluth homeowners call about most

Census estimates put Duluth at about 13,800 housing units, and roughly 58 percent of them went up between 1980 and 1999. The median year built is 1994. That is a city of houses and condos old enough to be on a second or third water heater.

The city's own long-range plan says Duluth built 3,667 housing units in the 1980s, and 2,151 of those were apartments. So a Duluth caller is as likely to live in a stacked condo or townhome as in a detached house, and the two get different plumbing problems.

A flooded utility closet at night is a water heater repair call, and a tank that has run for more than ten years is usually a candidate for water heater replacement instead. We take emergency calls in Duluth around the clock, and the first question is always the same: where is the water, and can the main be shut off?

Calls we expect from Duluth houses and condos

These are the problems that bring the most calls from a housing stock of this age. If one sounds like yours, the 24/7 emergency line is the right number at any hour.

Water stains under a tank

A rust ring or a damp pan under a water heater from the 1990s or later is the usual lead-in to a failed tank. Shut off the cold supply to it and call.

A ceiling stain below a condo bathroom

In stacked buildings the water often starts in the unit above. Tell the upstairs neighbor and the association, then call us if the leak is on your side of the wall.

Drains that gurgle together

When a tub, sink and toilet all complain at once, the blockage is usually in the main line, which is where drain cleaning with a cable or jet comes in.

A toilet that runs or rocks

Worn flappers, fill valves and loose flanges are common in houses where the original fixtures are still in use. A toilet repair visit often fixes it in one stop.

A water bill that jumped

A hidden leak shows up on the meter before it shows up on a wall. Leak detection finds where the water is going.

Who owns what: Gwinnett County, you, or your association

Duluth water and sewer service comes from Gwinnett County Water Resources, per the city's utilities page. The county's 24/7 dispatch line for water and sewer emergencies is 678.376.7000, and Customer Care is 678.376.6800. Call dispatch first if a main or a meter is leaking in the street.

Gwinnett County says the property owner maintains the private sewer lateral, and that everything on the house side of the water meter is the homeowner's responsibility. A cracked lateral or a broken line after the meter is a plumbing job, and sewer line repair covers the lateral when a camera confirms the damage.

Condo owners have a third party to consider. The association documents usually say whether the shared drain stack and supply risers belong to the building or to each unit. Read them before you pay for a repair inside a wall, and call us for the part that is yours.

Permits, inspections and Old Town

Plumbing is a trade permit in Duluth. The city's Community Development and Engineering Department, Permitting and Inspections Division, takes trades permits online through GovWell, and it can be reached at 770.476.1790 on weekdays. The city lists a slab plumbing inspection among its building inspections, so ask before any work that opens a slab.

Duluth City Hall sits at the end of the Town Green in Old Town Duluth, but you do not have to go downtown to file. A licensed contractor registers with the city and files the trades permit for the job. Permit rules vary by the kind of work, so ask your installer and the city before a heater or repipe job.

According to the City of Duluth's own planning FAQ, water and sewer code inspections are handled by Gwinnett County, and water or sewer emergencies go to Gwinnett County Water Resources at 678.376.7000. Trades permits, including plumbing, are filed through the city's GovWell portal.

Before We Get to Your Duluth Address

Find the main shutoff

In a detached house it is usually where the water line enters, and in a condo it may be inside the unit or in a shared closet. Turn it clockwise if water is running and nobody has stopped it.

Clear the water heater closet

Houses from the 1980s and 1990s often have the tank in a tight garage corner or hall closet. Move boxes so the plumber can reach the tank, the cold supply and the drain valve.

Ask about the building

In a condo or townhome, tell the association that a leak is active. Have the unit number, the management contact and any gate code ready when you call us.

Protect the floors

Lift rugs, cords and cardboard off any wet floor, and keep children away from outlets near the water. If water is near electrical panels, stay out and call.

Stop the drains

If a toilet or tub is backing up, stop flushing and skip the laundry and the dishwasher. Every cycle sends more water toward the blocked line.

Gas first

If a gas water heater or furnace is nearby and you smell gas, leave first. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside, then call us.
